I'm a disaster when it comes to writing frequently on this blog. Every time I say "This time I'm going to write consistently on the blog", you can rest assured that I won't do it. The funny thing is that when it comes to my personal diary I'm the complete opposite: I have been consistently journaling since 2011, so I have more than 15 years worth of journals. Sadly I've lost some of them while moving around here and there (needless to say, my journals are all handwritten), but I still treasure the majority of this fine (?) piece of art.

It is interesting to notice how I've changed through time. Keeping a journal is an exercise that everybody should do, if anything to realize that the idea of self to which we cling so desperately it's nothing but an illusion of our own mind. We are constantly changing, our ideas change, our opinions change, even how we remember things change; I've lost the count of how many times I was surprised to find in my journals something described in a completely different way from what I remember. Our brains are really marvelous things, distorting reality and memories only to protect our fragile egos. The paper of our journals is by no means an unbiased narrator of reality (if such a thing even exists), but it has the privilege of taking a snapshot of the things we live in the exact moment we live them, without changing them. If you think I'm lying to you and that your brain is a trusty narrator, well I invite you to run a journal in an unfiltered manner for at least one year and then read it back; you will so surprised you will barely believe you are the author.

Marco
